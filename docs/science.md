# Scientific basis: Clean Approach

## Problem formulation

**For a hypothetical Jezero sedimentary rock core reserved for later organic-molecule and potential-biosignature analysis, how can a crew preserve contact provenance when Earth-associated material on a glove or tool might reach the core through handling?**

The sample type and analysis are motivated by Perseverance's Sapphire Canyon sample, a 6.2 cm sedimentary core taken from the Cheyava Falls rock in Bright Angel. NASA reports that SHERLOC detected organic compounds there, and that organic compounds can also form through non-biological processes. The returned sample could support more detailed studies of its molecular and geological context. This is a *science-interpretation* problem: Earth-sourced organics or biological residues may complicate attribution or mask a Martian signal. The current map ties the PDS sample-report coordinate nominally to a valid CTX cell; its datum and location accuracy are unspecified, so it does not resolve the exact sampled surface. [NASA sample record](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/mars-rock-samples/), [SHERLOC / Bright Angel research](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09413-0).

This is distinct from **forward planetary protection**: preventing viable Earth organisms from surviving, establishing, or spreading on Mars. Nonviable cells, DNA or organic films can affect sample interpretation without establishing life; conversely, biological establishment is not inferred from the presence of an organic molecule. Mars 2020's biological cleanliness requirements and its returned-sample organic cleanliness requirements address related but distinct objectives. They are robotic mission requirements, not human EVA limits. [NASA Planetary Protection requirements](https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/planetary-protection/), [Mars 2020 sample-tube controls](https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2022.0049).

## Selected model and interpretation

The app now replays a qualitative, directed event history across possible human-associated material, glove G1, target-A material, tools T1/T2, target surfaces and sample B. Every contact edge belongs to an ordered event with a place, participants and written assumption. The output is which source IDs have a path to B, plus the events along each path. It has no units and is not a risk score, probability, transferred mass, viable-cell count or recommendation. Source presence and all future crew contacts are scenario inputs; suit/glove evidence from other settings does not show transfer on Mars.

The three same-route comparisons separate tool reuse from an explicitly modeled glove contact: Plan A uses T1 at both A and B; Plan B uses T2 at B but retains G1→T2; Plan C uses T2 and omits only G1→T2. The model finds A-origin and human-associated inputs reach B in A and B, while only B's own sample origin remains in C. This shows the effect of that declared edge in the scenario graph; it does not establish a physical control's effectiveness or make C an EVA recommendation. Sealing stores sample history; controls add documentation and remove no path.

The map's route/terrain measurements provide location context only and do not feed contact edges. The route is the same in all three plans.

Full variables, edge semantics, assumptions, code checks and the physical-validation gap are in [docs/model.md](model.md).

## Why not a quantitative contamination model?

A mass balance requires source loading, contacted area and transfer efficiencies for each interface, plus sample mass and an analytically defined contaminant. None is available for a Mars astronaut glove-to-core sequence. Rover values are hardware- and process-specific. No arbitrary transfer fractions or probability distributions are supplied. An airborne model would additionally require release rates, particle properties, local time/space winds, deposition and survival; MIASMMA is a research plan, not measured Mars EVA output. These omissions are material, not small uncertainty bars.

## Data-to-decision boundary

- **MRO/CTX DTM and orthoimage:** real 20.40 m/cell terrain and surface context; display the area and calculate illustrative route geometry only. They contain no glove contamination or biological transport measurement.
- **Mars 2020 Perseverance sample records / SHERLOC:** inform the sample type and why organic/biosignature interpretation matters. The sample-report coordinate is placed nominally on a valid cell of the current CTX crop (datum and horizontal accuracy unstated); the records supply no human transfer parameter.
- **Mars Science Laboratory / Curiosity SAM sample-chain work:** shows how spacecraft-contact residues and a cleaning sequence can affect organic measurements; its numeric values are not transplanted to EVA.
- **MEDA wind sample:** actual Sol 36 derived observations are available as a historical point series near the Perseverance landing point. Sapphire Canyon was collected on Sol 1215; its PDS coordinate maps nominally to the current CTX crop, though the report gives no datum or horizontal accuracy. The illustrative astronaut scenario has no matched date. The series is background only; it does not feed the contact graph or determine transfer. Wind can move aeolian dust/sand at Jezero, but human-associated release, particle transport, deposition onto a sample, and sample impact require additional mechanisms and measured inputs. [Newman et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abn3783) documents aeolian observations, not astronaut contamination.
- **MEDA temperature and UV/visible/IR radiation products:** archived, but not used. Temperature would need a defined crew/sample thermal decision and a thermal response model. Optical radiometry would need exposure duration, shielding/dust and material-specific survival or degradation response. These are not RAD-like ionizing-radiation measurements.
- **MOLA/HRSC/THEMIS:** not included; their broad terrain/mineral context would not fix the missing contact or airborne-transfer parameters.

The source-level metadata and limitations are tracked in [docs/data-sources.md](data-sources.md). The existing CTX crop and derivative remain unchanged.

The MEDA data show a concrete thing that was not previously observable: the historical wind speed and wind-from direction change during actual local-time observation windows near the landing site; a steady or route-wide vector would conceal that variation. They do **not** tell the user whether a hypothetical astronaut source plume reaches the core. The derived-wind index has no listed product for the Sapphire Canyon sampling sol, the target is not co-located with Sol 36's rover point, MEDA uncertainty/booms-used fields are empty in this product, and no human-particle release/deposition model exists. Recommendation: keep this data test and figure as scientific background; do not add a wind toggle or arrow to the app yet.

MEDA temperature products include calibrated air temperature (ATS) and calibrated/derived thermal-infrared products (TIRS); radiation/dust records include optical UV-to-near-IR measurements (RDS). These could support thermal exposure or photochemical exposure questions only after defining a decision and the missing suit/sample/material response parameters. Curiosity RAD is a distinct energetic-particle instrument in Gale, not MEDA optical irradiance and not local Jezero data.

## Scientific limitations

1. No direct human Mars-EVA-to-sample transfer measurement is available here.
2. The qualitative graph says a path is *possible under assumed contacts*; it cannot validate that the path occurs or that omitting an assumed contact prevents physical transfer.
3. The three outcomes change only where the listed event assumptions change. They do not rank operational plans or prove an intervention effect.
4. A map route is not a contact record. The drawn start, A/B locations and route are illustrative; no traversal, exposure, deposition or safety model is present.
5. The B marker is a nominal placement of the Sapphire Canyon sample-report coordinate on the CTX grid. The report states no datum or horizontal accuracy and the ~20.4 m posting cannot resolve the drill hole, so the marker must not be presented as the exact sampled spot.
6. Sample contamination control and preventing viable organisms from establishing on Mars are not interchangeable goals.
